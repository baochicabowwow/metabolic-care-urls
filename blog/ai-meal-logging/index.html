How I Use Claude to Log My Child’s Meals in Seconds

By Krestrel’s founder · Published 28 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Tell Claude what your child ate in plain words; it finds each food in your Krestrel food list and logs the meal.
  • It only logs foods it’s sure about, asks when a name is ambiguous, and says which amounts it estimated.
  • The meal counts toward today’s protein right away, and you can change or delete it in the app.
  • Claude can’t change protein ranges, formula, medicines or doses, and you choose exactly what it can see.

I built Krestrel because our kitchen had a notebook, a calculator, a kitchen scale and a phone, and all four were needed every time our child ate. The protein range from the metabolic clinic was simple on paper. Turning a messy toddler lunch into grams of protein, three times a day plus snacks, was not.

Krestrel made the arithmetic disappear. The part that still took effort was the tapping: search a food, pick raw or cooked, type the grams, repeat for every food on the plate, while a hungry toddler is throwing peas. These days, most of our meals are logged with one sentence to Claude. Here is what that looks like, and what happens behind it.

What does logging a meal with Claude look like?

This is an example conversation with a fictional child, Maya, using the same Krestrel tools a real connection uses.

Log lunch for Maya at 12:30: 40 g banana, 30 g white rice and a spoonful of sweet potato.

Logged lunch for Maya at 12:30:
• Banana, raw — 40 g, 0.4 g protein
• Rice, white, cooked — 30 g, 0.8 g
• Sweet potato, boiled — 15 g, 0.2 g (estimated: I used one tablespoon for “a spoonful”)

Lunch adds 1.4 g. Maya is at 7.8 g today. Her care team’s range is 10–12 g, so 2.2 g to the low end and 4.2 g before the top. You can change any amount in the Krestrel app.

What happens behind that one sentence?

  1. Claude looks each food up in your child’s Krestrel food list with the search foods tool. It gets the protein per 100 g and any named servings (“1 slice = 28 g”).
  2. It only logs foods it’s sure about. If “rice” could mean several foods, nothing is saved and Claude asks you which one. Close spellings are never logged without asking.
  3. It logs the meal with the log meal tool: the meal type, the time and each food with its grams. Where you didn’t give grams it uses the food’s usual serving, and it tells you which amounts were estimated.
  4. The meal lands in Krestrel exactly like one you logged by hand. It counts toward today’s protein, “g left today” updates on every caregiver’s phone, and you can change or delete it in the app.
  5. A new food needs a source. Claude can add a food that isn’t in your list only with a protein value and where it came from, such as the package label or a named reference. It never makes one up.

How do I get accurate results?

  • Give grams when you weighed it. “40 g banana” beats “some banana”. For the foods that carry most of the protein, keep using the scale.
  • Name the meal and the time, especially when you’re logging after the fact: “dinner at 6pm”.
  • Say raw or cooked when it matters. Cooked rice and dry rice are very different per gram.
  • Ask the follow-up. “How much protein is left today?” gets you the running balance against the range your care team set.
  • Glance at the app once the meal is logged, at least until you trust how your family’s phrases get matched.

What won’t Claude do?

  • It can’t change a protein range, formula, medicine or dose, and it won’t suggest changes. Those questions go to your care team.
  • It can’t edit or delete anything. Corrections happen in the app.
  • It only sees the children and areas you allowed. Logging meals needs Read & add on diet; you can give Read on labs and nothing at all on everything else.
  • Every request appears under Recent AI activity in Krestrel, and one switch turns AI apps off for the whole family.

How do I set it up?

It takes about three minutes: copy the connector address in Krestrel (Settings → AI & connected apps), add it as a custom connector in Claude, sign in, choose what Claude may see, and allow. The step-by-step guide for Claude has screenshots, and there is a guide for ChatGPT too. Read more about what the connection can and can’t do, or see meal logging in the app.

Frequently asked questions

Can Claude log a meal straight into Krestrel?

Yes. With Read & add access to diet, Claude looks each food up in your child’s Krestrel food list and logs the meal. It goes straight into the meal log and counts toward today’s protein, and you can change or delete it in the app.

What if Claude isn’t sure which food I meant?

It doesn’t guess. If a name could mean several foods, or only close spellings come back, nothing is saved and Claude asks you which one you meant.

What if a food isn’t in my child’s food list?

Claude can add it, but only with a protein value and where that number comes from, such as the package label or a named reference like USDA FoodData Central. It never invents a protein value.

Does this work with ChatGPT too?

Yes. The same Krestrel connection works in ChatGPT; the setup steps are slightly different.

Can Claude change my child’s protein range?

No. The connection has no tool that changes a protein range, formula, medicine or dose. Those stay between you and your care team.

Krestrel keeps the protein total, formula, labs and the whole care log in one place.
