Krestrel vs flok Health vs the Metabolic Diet App

By the Krestrel team · Published 28 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Krestrel is a free iPhone and iPad care log for families: protein against your dietitian’s range, formula, labs, growth, caregivers, an emergency plan, and Claude/ChatGPT connections.
  • flok Health (iPhone and Android) pairs diet tracking with mood, symptoms, wearables and optional research participation.
  • The Metabolic Diet App is a free web tool using MetabolicPro nutrient data, with an exportable food diary and broad disorder coverage.
  • All three are support tools; your metabolic team sets the diet.

If your child has an inherited metabolic disorder, you’ve probably been handed a protein number and a list of apps. Three come up often: Krestrel (ours), flok Health, and the Metabolic Diet App promoted by the Organic Acidemia Association. They overlap less than you’d think. This is a fair side-by-side, built only from what each publisher says publicly.

We make Krestrel, so read this with that in mind. Competitor details come from their app-store listings and publishers’ pages as of 28 November 2026; features change, so check the current listing before you choose. Corrections are welcome at support@krestrel.com.

What is Krestrel best for?

Families caring for a baby or young child day to day, where the metabolic diet sits inside everything else: bottles of metabolic formula, solids weighed on a scale, blood levels before each clinic visit, growth that changes the per-kg protein range, several caregivers logging into the same record, and an emergency letter ready for the ER. It also connects to Claude and ChatGPT for plain-language logging.

Limits to know: iPhone and iPad only; English only; no reminders yet; it tracks total protein, with amino acids shown as estimates from your dietitian’s factor rather than as separate limits.

What is flok Health best for?

People of all ages with protein-metabolism disorders who want diet tracking alongside mood, symptoms and wearable data, and who are interested in contributing to patient-led research. Its condition list includes tyrosinemia and urea cycle disorders, and it is on both iPhone and Android.

What is the Metabolic Diet App best for?

Families who want a free, browser-based tracker with no download, nutrient data from the MetabolicPro database used by metabolic dietitians, homemade recipes, and a food diary they can export for the clinic. It covers a broad set of disorders, including several organic acidemias.

Can I use more than one?

Yes, and some families do: for example, one app for the daily care log the whole household shares, and another for a specific purpose such as a research programme. Ask your metabolic dietitian which reports they find easiest to read.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best app for tracking a metabolic diet?

It depends on what you need. Krestrel focuses on whole-child daily care for families, including formula, labs, growth, caregivers and an emergency plan. flok Health adds symptom, mood and wearable tracking and optional research participation. The Metabolic Diet App is a free web tool with nutrient data from the MetabolicPro database and an exportable food diary.

Which app supports organic acidemias?

The Metabolic Diet App lists organic acidemias such as glutaric, isovaleric, methylmalonic and propionic acidemia. flok Health lists organic acidemias among its conditions. Krestrel tracks total protein against your dietitian’s range, which works for protein-restricted diets including GA1 and IVA.

Are these apps free?

Krestrel is free with no ads. flok Health is listed as free on the App Store. The Metabolic Diet App is described by the Organic Acidemia Association as free. Check each app’s current terms.

Do any of these apps replace my metabolic dietitian?

No. All three describe themselves as support or record-keeping tools. Your metabolic team sets the diet.
